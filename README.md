# Digital Heritage Portfolio

The scholarly value of a digital heritage record depends not only on the fidelity of its representation but also on whether the knowledge and evidence associated with its creation can be preserved and critically examined. The research presented in this portfolio investigates how three-dimensional and time-dependent heritage records can be connected to semantic descriptions, documented sources, and interfaces for scholarly interpretation. Through studies of tangible, intangible, and natural heritage, it considers how computational methods can support contextual understanding, transparent documentation, and the sustained reuse of digital resources.

**Research areas**: Digital Heritage, Digital Humanities, 3D/4D Heritage Documentation, Data Visualization, Knowledge Graph, Semantic Web, Artificial Intelligence, Digital Curation and Preservation

## Cross-cutting themes

| Theme | Where it is addressed |
|---|---|
| **3D/4D records as knowledge sites.** A 3D or 4D model is placed at the centre and contextualised through annotations, narratives and linked records. | Annotations keyed to frames of a volumetric recording ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/)); movements addressable by frame range, with curated interpretive paths ([02](02_chunaengjeon-4d/)); specimens in Smithsonian Voyager with their digitisation lineage ([03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)); building elements linked to a knowledge graph by ID ([06](06_tse-collaborative-environment/)) |
| **Paradata and interpretive uncertainty.** Modelling and interpretive decisions are recorded with the record, and uncertainty is shown rather than smoothed over. | Unverified annotations and calibration decisions kept as paradata ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/)); open questions flagged in the interface ([02](02_chunaengjeon-4d/)); remains, structural inferences and comparative inferences distinguished, with gaps in the source data kept visible ([06](06_tse-collaborative-environment/)); generated images kept apart from collection records ([05](05_generative-heritage-exploration/)) |
| **From user requirements to specifications.** The needs of scholars, experts and users are turned into design attributes and testable requirements. | Expert interviews and Kano–QFD analysis turned into prioritised design attributes ([03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)); FAIR indicators turned into testable specifications ([07](07_fair-assessment/)); a shared environment for architectural historians, archaeologists and digitisation specialists ([06](06_tse-collaborative-environment/)) |
| **Web delivery of 3D/4D data.** Capture data are optimised and rendered in the browser. | Per-frame 3DGS packed into a quantised web sequence (about 3.4 MB per frame) and decoded by a WebGL2 player ([02](02_chunaengjeon-4d/)); Gaussian-splat, volumetric and glTF components combined in one scene ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/)); Draco-compressed glTF specimens ([04](04_nhiv-3d-specimen-maps/)); Voyager Explorer embedded in a research interface ([03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)) |
| **FAIR publication and preservation.** Provisions are implemented and verified against a running service. | Content negotiation, SHACL validation, a DCAT catalogue and fixity checks in a deployed archive ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/)); indicator-level assessments from design to deployment ([07](07_fair-assessment/)); published camera parameters and open PLY derivatives ([02](02_chunaengjeon-4d/)) |
| **Evaluation of research value and user experience.** Each system is assessed for what it supports and for how users experience it. | Competency questions ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/), [03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)); ranking analyses with a reproducibility package ([03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)); expert assessment and user studies ([05](05_generative-heritage-exploration/), [03](03_nexus-natural-heritage/)); usability instrumentation ([01](01_hansan-mosi-scene-archive/)) |

## Methods, Standards, and Tools

### Semantic Modelling and Knowledge Representation

Heritage information is modelled through domain-specific entities and relationships to make the context of objects, activities, people, and digital records computationally accessible. Implementations use knowledge graphs and Neo4j/Cypher, together with CIDOC CRM-aligned modelling, RDF/Turtle and JSON-LD serialisation, and SHACL-based validation. EDM, Dublin Core, LIDO, W3C PROV, and Darwin Core inform selected mappings or metadata design decisions where appropriate; their inclusion does not imply full implementation in every project.

### 3D/4D Documentation and Computational Visualisation

The reconstruction workflow draws on photogrammetry and volumetric capture data, camera registration, and time-dependent 3D Gaussian Splatting using gsplat. Geometric and animated resources are processed or exchanged through formats including Alembic, PLY, and glTF/GLB, with Blender supporting 3D data preparation and inspection. Web-based visualisation employs Three.js and WebGL2 to connect spatial representations with annotations and temporally defined segments of recorded activity.

### AI-Assisted Information Exploration

AI methods are investigated as means of supporting the discovery, organisation, and interpretation of heritage information while maintaining a distinction between source evidence and model-generated outputs. The research works with language models together with vision and multimodal models: CLIP-based multimodal representations, GraphSAGE for graph-based inference, FLUX.1 LoRA fine-tuning for visual exploration, and language models that ground prompts and reuse suggestions in documented records. Generated outputs are kept apart from collection records and are reviewed by a pool of heritage experts.

### Research Interfaces and Evaluation

Interactive research applications are developed using Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, React, Vite, and Next.js, supported by relational and graph databases such as PostgreSQL/Supabase, SQLite, and Neo4j. Evaluation methods are selected according to the research question and include competency-question validation, quantitative analysis of recommendation behaviour, expert assessment, and user studies. Where evaluation instruments are implemented but data collection remains incomplete, that distinction is documented in the relevant project.

### Digital Curation, Provenance, and Reproducibility

Data stewardship is addressed through explicit provenance records, version information, rights and licensing metadata, format documentation, and SHA-256 fixity verification. DCAT and schema.org Dataset descriptions support machine-readable resource discovery, while the RDA FAIR Data Maturity Model provides a framework for assessing the operational status of FAIR provisions. Persistent-identifier registration, access procedures, and long-term preservation arrangements are distinguished from features already implemented and verified.

## Repository Scope and Attribution

This repository presents selected research implementations and their supporting materials rather than complete project archives. Publicly available resources include case-study descriptions, visualisations, metadata models, annotation and validation materials, reconstruction code, and documented reproducibility packages. Raw volumetric captures, personal information, licensed data, and some unpublished research resources remain in their respective project environments. Individual project pages specify the evidence that can be inspected and the limitations of its release.
